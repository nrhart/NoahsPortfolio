Pac-Man

A browser build of my original openFrameworks C++ project. Play a Pac-Man-inspired game combining ghost AI, A* pathfinding, and procedural maze generation.

Use arrow keys, WASD, or the direction buttons. Collect pellets and power pellets while avoiding the ghosts. Choose Procedural Maze, then Restart / New Maze for another generation attempt. The generator checks reachability and can fall back to the classic layout.

Development Process

For my Game AI final, I returned to an idea from my undergraduate coursework: Pac-Man as a setting for exploring agent behavior. This time, I built on my movement and pathfinding assignments to create a playable C++ project with a custom behavior tree and a procedurally generated maze.

The earlier assignments taught me to tune predictable movement, compare pathfinding heuristics, and organize decisions into extensible systems. Debugging agents that became stuck or switched states too quickly showed me how closely movement, navigation, and decision making depend on one another. Pac-Man became a way to apply those lessons together: ghost behavior trees choose a mode, distinct targeting rules establish a destination, and A* supplies the route.

I extended that foundation with procedural maze generation, using randomized backtracking, mirrored layouts, and reachability checks to connect generated environments to the same gameplay and navigation systems. The project reflects my approach throughout the course: keep building on earlier work, choose techniques that fit the problem, and make the reasoning behind the implementation clear.
